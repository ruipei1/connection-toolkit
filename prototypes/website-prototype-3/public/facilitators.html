The guide draws on the supplied Stanford curriculum as a reference. Adapt local examples and discuss research claims in context.

  1. Notice expectations · 15 minutes
    Set an optional participation norm. Reflect privately, then discuss expectations in pairs.
  2. Get curious · 15 minutes
    Introduce the research and explore plausible interpretations of an ambiguous situation.
  3. Plan a small step · 15 minutes
    Choose a manageable action, decide when to try it, and close with a reflection.

Before you begin

A little preparation
makes more room.

Facilitation principles

Keep disclosure optional.

Invite students to work with everyday situations. They can write privately, choose a fictional example, or pass on sharing.

Make room for mixed experiences.

A disappointing interaction can happen. Ask what students observed and what they learned, without insisting on a positive interpretation.

Let the group’s responses surprise you.

A perception gap does not have to appear for an activity to be useful. Accurate expectations and differences across situations can also start a worthwhile discussion.

Preserve the purpose as you adapt.

Keep the progression from expectations to curiosity to action. Distinguish campus-specific findings from claims about your own students.
