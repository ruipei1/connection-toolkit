FROM PERCEPTION TO CONNECTION

A workshop in three parts

Audience: College students in a classroom, orientation group, or residence. Time: About 45 minutes. Materials: Paper and pens; optional live survey.

Before you begin

Make participation and personal disclosure optional. Invite everyday examples and leave room for mixed or disappointing experiences. Adapt local language; do not apply Stanford statistics to your campus.

1. Notice expectations · 15 minutes

Ask students to privately consider how willing they would be to welcome a conversation or listen to a classmate. Then ask them to estimate peers’ willingness. Discuss in pairs: What shaped your estimates? How might those expectations affect what you do?

2. Get curious · 15 minutes

Introduce the empathy perception gap: peers may describe themselves as more caring than we expect. This is a group-level pattern, not a guarantee about a particular person. Use an ambiguous situation: “A classmate declines lunch without suggesting another time.” Generate several plausible explanations. What did you observe? What did you infer? What could you ask or do to learn more?

3. Plan a small step · 15 minutes

Invite students to choose a manageable action: say hello, express appreciation, invite someone to coffee, or ask for support. Write the action and a specific time. Pair sharing is optional. Close with: “What is one social assumption you could test this week?”

Follow-up

What did you predict? What happened? What did you learn? Respect a no and avoid treating one interaction as a verdict on future connections.
